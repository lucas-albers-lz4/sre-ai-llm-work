## Principles

- New section: "your users, not your monitoring, decide your reliability" — choose metrics by what users experience, not what the stack measures easily
- New section: the error budget is the dev/SRE wall-breaker — Evernote's SLO-centric model gives both teams a shared fact base without merging roles
- New section: complexity is an externality — designate a whole-system champion, since the team that introduces complexity never pays its cost
- New rule: align incentives with an explicit safety pledge — "support you in releasing as quickly as is safe," with the error budget as the definition of "safe"

## Incident Response
- Chapter promoted from stub: full incident-response doctrine — mitigate vs. coordinate split, IC/CL/OL role hierarchy, declare-early-and-often
- New rule: split an AI responder into mitigation and coordination — a responder that insists on root-cause before acting is an analyst, not a first responder

## Runbooks and Agents
- New rule: write runbooks as executable spec — exact commands, named steps, explicit decision points; a human-only runbook is not an automation input
- New automation-safety baseline: risk-gate every agent action, reads included, and route to a human on anything the check can't clear
- New rules: decompose human workflows rather than transcribe them; agent automation is a long-lived liability with an error budget of its own
- New rule: structured typed tool contracts over free-form dicts — a "here's a dict, figure it out" schema shifts its complexity onto every calling agent
- New rule: AI incident agent as scribe and timeline reconstructor of the PagerDuty source-of-truth/ledger/bridge topology — don't replace the human decision bridge
